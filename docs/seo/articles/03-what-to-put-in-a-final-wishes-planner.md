# What to Put in a Final Wishes Planner

A final wishes planner is a single place where you write down what you want for your health care, your belongings and your send-off, so your family does not have to guess. It is not a legal document on its own. It works best alongside your will and any other legal papers you have.

Here is what most people include, and the details that are easiest to forget.

## Medical wishes

- Your current medications, dosages and allergies.
- Past surgeries and serious conditions.
- Your preferred doctors, hospital and pharmacy.
- Your wishes for treatment if you become seriously ill or unable to decide for yourself.
- The name of the person you want to speak for you, and where any health care directive is stored.

Talk to your doctor and that person about your wishes, so they understand what you mean.

## Money and accounts

- Bank, retirement and investment accounts, and the institution for each.
- Bills, subscriptions and automatic payments.
- Who manages each account, and where to find statements.
- Debts and loans.

Do not write passwords into the planner. Record where your family can find them securely, such as in a password manager or a sealed envelope held by your attorney.

## Insurance, wills and trusts

- Life, health, home, car and long-term care policies, with the company and policy number.
- Where your will and any trust documents are kept.
- Your beneficiaries, and whether the details are up to date.

## Home and service contacts

- The people who keep your home running: the handyman, the HVAC company, the plumber and the lawn service.
- The location of keys, the water shut-off and the fuse box.

## Funeral and final arrangements

- Your wishes for a service, burial or cremation.
- Any prepaid funeral or cemetery arrangements, and where the paperwork is kept.
- Music, readings or people you would like involved.

## Digital life

Families often forget these:
- Email accounts and phone accounts.
- Social media accounts, and whether you want them memorialised or closed.
- Cloud storage and photo libraries.
- Subscriptions that keep charging after you are gone.

## Life story

A planner is also a place for the things money cannot record: the stories, values and advice you want to leave behind. Many families value these pages most of all.

## A few common mistakes

- Writing the plan once and never updating it.
- Keeping the planner somewhere no one can find.
- Assuming a trusted person knows the details without being told.

Review your planner once a year, and after any major change in your life.

## Questions people ask

**Is a final wishes planner legally binding?**
Not by itself. It records your wishes and details. Legal documents, such as a will, health care directive and powers of attorney, are what make wishes binding. Speak with a qualified attorney in your state.

**Where should I keep it?**
Somewhere safe, with at least one trusted person who knows where it is.
