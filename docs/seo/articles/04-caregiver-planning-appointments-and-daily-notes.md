# Caregiver Planning: Keeping Appointments and Daily Notes in One Place

Caring for an older parent, partner or friend often means keeping track of many things at once: appointments, medications, meals, moods, visitors and the questions to ask at the next doctor visit. When those details live in different phones, notes apps and paper scraps, important things get missed.

A single caregiver planner solves much of this. It gives you one place to look before every appointment and one place to share updates with the rest of the family.

## What caregivers usually track

- **Appointments:** the date, time, location, doctor and the reason for the visit.
- **Medications:** the name, dose, time taken and any side effects noticed.
- **Daily notes:** sleep, appetite, mood, mobility and any changes from usual.
- **Questions:** things to raise with the doctor before the next visit.
- **Contacts:** doctors, pharmacy, neighbours, transport and other helpers.

## A simple daily routine

1. **Morning (two minutes):** record medications taken and any overnight changes.
2. **Midday (two minutes):** note meals, fluids and anything unusual.
3. **Evening (five minutes):** write one line about the day and plan tomorrow's appointments or tasks.

Keep the routine short. A planner you use every day is more useful than a detailed one you stop filling in.

## Sharing notes with family

Choose one person to be the main contact for appointments. Share the planner, or a photo of the week's page, with the rest of the family once a week. Agree on what counts as urgent and who should be called first.

## Bringing the planner to appointments

Take the planner to every doctor visit. Show the last few weeks of notes, the medication list and your questions. This helps the doctor see patterns you might not notice day to day.

## Keeping caregiving sustainable

Caregivers need rest too. Write down the respite options you have, the people who can step in, and the times you need a break. Writing it down makes it easier to ask for help.

## Questions caregivers ask

**Should I use a printed planner or an app?**
Either works. A printed planner is easy to share with people who are not comfortable with apps, and it does not need charging. Use whichever one you will actually fill in every day.

**How much detail is enough?**
Enough that someone else could step in for a day without calling you. Start small and add detail as you need it.

**What if the person I care for does not want me to track things?**
Talk with them about why the notes help. Many people feel more in control when they can see that their care is planned.
