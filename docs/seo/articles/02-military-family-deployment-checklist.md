# A Deployment Checklist for Military Families

A deployment, training rotation or long assignment away from home puts a lot on the family who stays behind. The service member is focused on the mission. The spouse, parents or caregiver are left to handle appointments, bills, children and the house, often with little warning.

A written plan makes that easier for everyone. This checklist covers the areas that families most often ask about. Use it as a starting point, and check your unit's family readiness guidance for requirements that apply to you.

## Before you leave

**Documents**
- Gather identification, birth certificates, marriage certificate and military records. Keep copies in a place your family can find.
- Record where your will, powers of attorney and any trust documents are kept.
- Confirm who is listed as an emergency contact with your unit and your insurer.

**Medical**
- List allergies, current medications and dosages, and any ongoing treatment.
- Record your doctors, pharmacy and preferred hospital.
- Note any family members with medical needs, and where their records are kept.
- Write down your medical preferences in case you cannot speak for yourself.

**Finances**
- List your bank accounts, bills and automatic payments, and when each one is due.
- Check who has access to each account, and whether a shared or joint account is needed.
- Record your insurance policies, and who to contact about each one.
- Plan how your pay and allowances will reach the household.

## Household and children

- Write down the routines for school, activities and childcare, and the people who help with them.
- List home and service contacts: the handyman, the HVAC company, the car mechanic and the internet provider.
- Note the plan for pets, including vet details and food.
- Leave a short letter or note for your children, if that feels right for your family.

## Keeping in touch

- Agree on how often you will talk, and what time zone works for both of you.
- Decide who the family should contact first in an urgent situation.
- Keep a shared list of questions to discuss on each call.

## When you return

Review the plan together. Update the contacts, bills and medical details that changed, and note anything that worked well for the next deployment.

## Questions families ask

**Does this replace official military family support?**
No. This checklist helps you organise your own household. Your unit's family readiness group and legal assistance office can help with official requirements.

**What if my spouse or caregiver has never handled the bills?**
Walk through each account together before you leave. Write down the login process and the due dates, and keep passwords in a secure place.

**What should a temporary caregiver know first?**
Start with the medical details, emergency contacts and the list of bills that need paying. Then cover routines and the home.
